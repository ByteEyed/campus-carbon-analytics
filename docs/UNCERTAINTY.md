## 1. Purpose & Executive Rationale

Deterministic forecasts provide a single projected trajectory of future carbon emissions (e.g., predicting that campus emissions in March 2025 will be exactly $133,799.57\text{ kgCO}_2\text{e}$). While computationally convenient, point forecasts create a dangerous illusion of certainty. In reality, campus decarbonization planning operates under multiple compounding layers of error:

1. **Statistical Unpredictability:** Random macroeconomic, weather, and operational shocks that deviate from the underlying Holt-Winters trend and seasonal parameters.
2. **Measurement Error:** Sensor calibration drift, estimated commuter mileage surveys, and unmetered campus building loads.
3. **Scientific & Geographic Factor Error:** Grid emission factors and global warming potentials published by governmental authorities represent regional averages with non-trivial margins of error.

Without quantifying uncertainty, university leadership cannot evaluate institutional compliance risk against climate targets or prioritize capital investments effectively. 

### Core Objectives of Phase 12:
- Introduce a **Monte Carlo Sensitivity Framework** wrapping the existing Phase 8 Carbon Accounting and Phase 11 Holt-Winters forecasting engines without altering core code.
- Quantify empirical 95% Confidence Bounds for the 2025 forecast horizon.
- Implement **One-at-a-Time (OAT) Variance Decomposition** to mathematically isolate and rank the three primary sources of uncertainty.

## 2. Mathematical Methodology

Analytically propagating measurement error through the multi-parameter $L\text{-BFGS-B}$ non-linear optimization of Holt-Winters exponential smoothing is mathematically intractable. Therefore, Phase 12 implements a **Monte Carlo Pipeline Wrapper with One-at-a-Time (OAT) Variance Decomposition**.

```
[Raw Activity Data]        [Emission Factor Registry]
       │                               │
       ▼                               ▼
  Perturbation                    Perturbation
   (sigma_act)                     (sigma_ef)
       │                               │
       └───────────────┬───────────────┘
                       ▼
           [Carbon Accounting Engine]
           (calculate_campus_emissions)
                       │
                       ▼
           [Monthly Date Aggregation]
           (aggregate_emissions_by_date)
                       │
                       ▼
           [Holt-Winters Forecaster]
           (additive trend + seasonality)
                       │
                       ▼
           [Monte Carlo Distributions]
         (95% CI & Variance Decomposition)
```

### 2.1 The Three Isolated Uncertainty Components

#### Component 1: Forecast Uncertainty ($V_{\text{forecast}}$)
Forecast uncertainty represents the intrinsic statistical variance of future states given fixed historical observations. We reuse the Phase 11 Holt-Winters state-space innovation simulation:
$$\hat{y}_{t+h} = \ell_{t+h-1} + b_{t+h-1} + s_{t+h-m} + \varepsilon_{t+h}$$
Using $N = 1000$ simulated forward paths generated by `fitted_model.simulate(nsimulations=12, repetitions=1000, error="add")`, the step-wise variance across simulated paths is computed:
$$V_{\text{forecast}}(h) = \frac{1}{N - 1} \sum_{k=1}^N \left( \hat{y}_{h, k} - \bar{y}_h \right)^2$$
The scalar forecast variance $V_{\text{forecast}}$ is the mean step-wise variance over the 12-month horizon $H$:
$$V_{\text{forecast}} = \frac{1}{H} \sum_{h=1}^H V_{\text{forecast}}(h)$$

#### Component 2: Activity-Data Uncertainty ($V_{\text{act}}$)
Activity uncertainty captures measurement error in physical campus operations (kWh, km, kg waste, INR). To isolate this component:
1. The forecast paths are frozen (point forecasts are evaluated).
2. Emission factors are held strictly fixed at baseline values ($\sigma_{\text{ef}} = 0$).
3. Historical activity data observations $X_{i, t}$ are independently perturbed across $N = 1000$ iterations.
4. For each perturbed history $k$, emissions are calculated, aggregated, and fitted with Holt-Winters to produce a deterministic point forecast vector $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$.
5. The variance of the point forecasts across simulations is computed:
$$V_{\text{act}}(h) = \frac{1}{N - 1} \sum_{k=1}^N \left( \hat{y}_{h, k}^{(\text{act})} - \bar{y}_h^{(\text{act})} \right)^2, \quad V_{\text{act}} = \frac{1}{H} \sum_{h=1}^H V_{\text{act}}(h)$$

#### Component 3: Emission-Factor Uncertainty ($V_{\text{ef}}$)
Emission factor uncertainty captures scientific and regional uncertainty in GHG conversion multipliers. To isolate this component:
1. Forecast paths are frozen (point forecasts are evaluated).
2. Historical activity data is held strictly fixed at unperturbed baseline values ($\sigma_{\text{act}} = 0$).
3. The emission factor registry is perturbed across $N = 1000$ iterations. Since emission factors represent institutional multipliers, each trial $k$ draws one perturbed factor per category applied uniformly across the entire history.
4. Holt-Winters is fitted to each resulting history to produce point forecast $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$.
5. The variance of point forecasts across simulations is computed:
$$V_{\text{ef}}(h) = \frac{1}{N - 1} \sum_{k=1}^N \left( \hat{y}_{h, k}^{(\text{ef})} - \bar{y}_h^{(\text{ef})} \right)^2, \quad V_{\text{ef}} = \frac{1}{H} \sum_{h=1}^H V_{\text{ef}}(h)$$

### 2.2 Decomposition & Normalization
Assuming independence between the three sources of error under the OAT framework, total variance is defined as:
$$V_{\text{total}} = V_{\text{forecast}} + V_{\text{act}} + V_{\text{ef}}$$
The normalized percentage contribution of each source is calculated as:
$$P_{\text{forecast}} = \left( \frac{V_{\text{forecast}}}{V_{\text{total}}} \right) \times 100\%$$
$$P_{\text{act}} = \left( \frac{V_{\text{act}}}{V_{\text{total}}} \right) \times 100\%$$
$$P_{\text{ef}} = \left( \frac{V_{\text{ef}}}{V_{\text{total}}} \right) \times 100\%$$
where $P_{\text{forecast}} + P_{\text{act}} + P_{\text{ef}} \equiv 100.0\%$.

## 3. Probability Distributions & Simulated Parameter Assumptions

> [!CAUTION]
> ### CRITICAL DISCLAIMER: SYNTHETIC PARAMETER ASSUMPTIONS
> The relative standard deviations (RSD) documented below are **simulated prototype assumptions** chosen for this undergraduate academic demonstration (TRL 4). They are based on plausible literature heuristics (IPCC Good Practice Guidance, GHG Protocol Scope 2 guidance) but **DO NOT** represent empirically measured campus instrument variances or audited utility meter calibration logs. These numbers must not be used for statutory compliance reporting without empirical on-site sensor validation.

### 3.1 Truncated Normal Formulation
Greenhouse gas activity data and emission factors are strictly non-negative physical quantities ($X \ge 0, EF \ge 0$). Applying unbounded Gaussian noise could generate negative electricity consumption or negative carbon conversion factors during tail draws.

To enforce physical laws, perturbations are drawn from a **Truncated Normal Distribution** bounded below at zero:
$$X'_{i, t} = \max\left(0.0, \, X_{i, t} \cdot (1 + Z_{i, t})\right), \quad Z_{i, t} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{act}, i}^2)$$
$$EF'_i = \max\left(0.0, \, EF_i \cdot (1 + Z_i)\right), \quad Z_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{ef}, i}^2)$$

## 5. Detailed Scientific Analysis of Findings

### 5.1 Why Emission Factor Uncertainty Dominates ($74.5\%$ – $87.5\%$)
In all three targets, emission factor uncertainty is by far the largest single contributor to total variance. This empirical outcome reflects standard greenhouse gas accounting theory:
1. **Systematic Multiplier Effect:** In carbon accounting, the emission factor is an institutional constant that scales the entire historical activity series:
   $$E_{\text{monthly}} = \text{Activity} \times EF$$
   When $EF$ fluctuates by $\pm 5\%$ or $\pm 20\%$, the entire time series shifts up or down synchronously. This induces a substantial shift in the Holt-Winters baseline level ($\ell_0$), which propagates linearly into all future forecasts.
2. **Travel EF Volatility:** In travel emissions, EF uncertainty accounts for **87.54%** of total variance. This is directly attributable to the large assumed $\sigma_{\text{ef}} = 20\%$. In passenger transit, the real-world carbon intensity per passenger-kilometer varies by up to $300\%$ depending on whether a commuter drives alone in an SUV, rides a two-wheeler, or uses an electric bus.

### 5.2 Why Activity Data Uncertainty is Modest ($8.3\%$ – $11.1\%$)
Despite assigning up to $15\%$ relative standard deviation to travel survey data, activity uncertainty contributes only $8.34\%$ of total variance for total emissions.
- **Law of Large Numbers & Central Limit Theorem:** Historical activity data is recorded across 5 distinct campus facilities and 24 monthly periods ($5 \times 24 = 120$ independent records per category). Because activity measurement errors are independent across buildings and time, positive errors partially cancel out negative errors when aggregated to campus-wide monthly totals.
- **Aggregated Smoothing:** When Holt-Winters is fitted to the smoothed monthly aggregates, the high-frequency measurement noise is attenuated by the smoothing equations ($\alpha \approx 0.55$).

### 5.3 Forecast State-Space Uncertainty ($2.0\%$ – $14.4\%$)
Forecast state-space uncertainty reflects the residual error ($\sigma_{\varepsilon}^2$) of the Holt-Winters model on unperturbed data.
- For **Electricity Emissions**, forecast uncertainty accounts for **14.39%** of variance because electricity consumption features strong weather-driven variations (heat waves, air conditioning demands) that Holt-Winters captures with minor residual variance.
- For **Travel Emissions**, forecast uncertainty is only **2.00%** of variance because the academic schedule (semester terms vs. vacation lulls) is extraordinarily regular and predictable, leaving very little unexplained residual variance.

## 7. Institutional Decision-Making Implications

For university management and sustainability officers, these findings deliver immediate strategic guidance:

1. **Prioritize Emission Factor Accuracy over Sub-Metering:**
   - Universities often spend significant capital installing sub-meters in every individual department to eliminate activity data errors.
   - However, our variance decomposition demonstrates that activity data error accounts for only **8.3%** of forecast uncertainty, whereas emission factor uncertainty accounts for **78.5%**.
   - **Recommendation:** Rather than spending large budgets on sub-meters, the campus should prioritize procuring verified, supplier-specific electricity emission factors (Scope 2 market-based accounting) and conduct empirical vehicle-type audits for campus commuter travel. Improving emission factor accuracy by $50\%$ reduces overall institutional planning risk by over $39\%$.
2. **Budgeting for Climate Buffers:**
   - In peak emissions months (e.g., April 2025 at $138.5\text{ MTCO}_2\text{e}$ point forecast), the true 95% upper bound reaches $155.2\text{ MTCO}_2\text{e}$ ($+12.0\%$).
   - Any contractual carbon offset purchase or net-zero budget must maintain an operational risk buffer of at least **12% to 15%** above point forecasts to ensure compliance.

## 8. Risks, Assumptions, and Methodological Limitations

1. **Uncorrelated Error Assumption:** The One-at-a-Time framework assumes that measurement errors in electricity meters are independent of errors in travel surveys or grid emission factors. In reality, macro-factors (e.g., severe grid disruptions or campus-wide power outages) could introduce cross-category correlations.
2. **Truncated Normal Distribution Assumption:** Truncated Gaussian distributions were chosen for analytical simplicity and robustness at TRL 4. Real-world emission factor uncertainties (particularly in fugitive methane from waste or agricultural procurement) frequently exhibit heavy-tailed Log-Normal distributions.
3. **Small Sample Test Validation:** While the Phase 11 Holt-Winters model achieved 100% empirical coverage on the 12-month test set, this is a small-sample sample artifact ($N = 12$). True long-term multi-year coverage requires continuous rolling-window evaluation.
4. **Computation Time & Dashboard Scalability:** While a single $N=1000$ simulation executes in ~18 seconds, executing full variance decomposition across multiple targets requires ~3.5 minutes. For interactive web dashboards (Phase 14), pre-computed simulation caches or lower iteration counts ($N=200$) should be utilized.
